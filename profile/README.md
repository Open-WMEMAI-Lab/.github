# Open-WMEMAI-Lab

**World Models · Embodied Intelligence · General Machine Intelligence**

Open-WMEMAI-Lab is my independent research initiative focused on a fundamental question: **how can intelligent agents learn the structure and dynamics of the world from experience, then use that knowledge to reason, plan, and act beyond the situations they have encountered?** I am interested in self-supervised, structured, and action-conditioned world models that learn from high-dimensional, continuous, and noisy observations, with particular emphasis on causal abstraction, compositional generalization, and transferable knowledge.

My long-term research objective is to advance the foundations of **human- and animal-like general intelligence** through embodied agents that continually build and revise internal models, retain and reuse knowledge, discover the consequences of actions, and compose learned capabilities to solve unfamiliar problems. I aim to investigate how predictive learning and world-theory discovery can be brought together with closed-loop planning and control—especially when environments change, observations are incomplete, or a model's predictions become unreliable. **Adaptation, uncertainty, and safety are central research challenges, not afterthoughts.**

I am also interested in **AI scientists** that can formulate mechanistic hypotheses, design informative experiments, evaluate competing explanations, and revise their models in response to evidence.

### Research Interests

- **Self-Supervised Predictive World Models** — Joint-embedding predictive architectures, energy-based learning, and action-conditioned latent dynamics.
- **Causal and Compositional World Understanding** — Object-centric representations, reusable abstractions, learned world theories, and systematic generalization.
- **Reasoning and Model-Based Planning** — Predicting action consequences, planning over learned models, and composing skills for novel goals.
- **Embodied Physical Intelligence** — Learning and acting from continuous sensorimotor experience, with an emphasis on transferable robotic capabilities.
- **Lifelong Learning, Uncertainty, and Safety** — Continual adaptation, persistent knowledge, detecting model inadequacy, and reliable autonomous behavior under distribution shift.
- **AI Scientists** — Hypothesis formation, experimental design, causal or mechanistic discovery, and evidence-driven model revision.

### Research Principles

I pursue fundamental research questions through careful engagement with prior work, reproducible experiments, strong and comparable baselines, controlled ablations, and evaluation under distribution shift. I distinguish research hypotheses from demonstrated findings, document limitations and negative results, and avoid unsupported claims of novelty or performance.

### Research and Development

I intend to publish selected research implementations, experimental benchmarks, and reproducibility materials as they become ready for public release. My aim is to contribute principled methods and credible empirical evidence toward intelligent systems that **learn how the world works—and use that understanding to reason and act.**
